How I read a cloth

Six small steps. No cloud queue.

This is a local read, not a render farm. The whole thing happens in your browser.

01

You bring the cloth.

A photo or a short video. A close-up in daylight is best.

02

I read the colour.

Five tones, pulled from the pixels, named in plain language.

03

I feel the fall.

Is it crisp, fluid, or somewhere in between? That decides the cut.

04

I sketch the rail.

Eight modern pieces and ten desi pieces, all from the same cloth.

05

I dress the figure.

An anime-style model study, so you can see the shape before you cut.

06

I write it down.

A short note on what the cloth could become, and what to keep close.

Cloth notes

A small library of fabrics I keep coming back to.

Every swatch gets the same read: colour, surface, fall, pieces and a note. These are the three shelves I reach for most.

01 · everyday

Linen & cotton

For shirts, summer dresses and soft trousers. The kind of cloth that gets better with a wash.

02 · structured

Wool & denim

For suits, jackets and wide trousers. It holds a line, and a little weather.

03 · occasion

Silk & velvet

For a long dress, a bias skirt, an evening jacket. Let the cloth move; keep the rest quiet.
